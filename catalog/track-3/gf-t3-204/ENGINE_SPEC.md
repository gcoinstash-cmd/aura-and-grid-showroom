Almgren-Chriss Optimal Liquidation Trajectory (Gate 1) ### 2.1 The Optimal Execution Problem Let a trader hold an initial inventory $X_0$ shares of an asset to be liquidated completely over a time horizon $T$ divided into $N$ discrete intervals of duration $\tau = T / N$ at discrete times $t_k = k \tau, k = 0, \dots, N$. The trajectory of remaining inventory is $x_k = x(t_k)$ with boundary conditions: $$x_0 = X_0, \quad x_N = 0$$ The number of shares liquidated during step $k$ is: $$n_k = x_{k-1} - x_k, \quad \sum_{k=1}^N n_k = X_0$$ ### 2.2 Price Dynamics & Market Impact The asset price $S_k$ follows an arithmetic Brownian motion with both permanent and temporary market impact: 1. **Permanent Market Impact $g(v)$**: $$S_k = S_{k-1} + \sigma \tau^{1/2} \xi_k - \tau g\left(\frac{n_k}{\tau}\right)$$ Assuming a linear permanent impact function with parameter $\gamma > 0$: $$g(v) = \gamma v \implies S_k = S_{k-1} + \sigma \tau^{1/2} \xi_k - \gamma n_k$$ 2. **Temporary Market Impact $h(v)$**: The effective execution price $\tilde{S}_k$ achieved on slice $n_k$ suffers temporary liquidity concession: $$\tilde{S}_k = S_{k-1} - h\left(\frac{n_k}{\tau}\right)$$ With linear temporary impact parameter $\eta > 0$: $$h(v) = \eta v \implies \tilde{S}_k = S_{k-1} - \frac{\eta}{\tau} n_k$$ ### 2.3 Optimization Objective: Expected Cost and Variance Total capture from liquidation is: $$R = \sum_{k=1}^N n_k \tilde{S}_k$$ Total Implementation Shortfall (cost relative to initial arrival price $S_0$) is: $$x = X_0 S_0 - R$$ The expected cost $E[x]$ and variance $V[x]$ are: $$E[x] = \frac{1}{2} \gamma X_0^2 + \frac{\eta}{\tau} \sum_{k=1}^N n_k^2$$ $$V[x] = \sigma^2 \sum_{k=1}^N \tau x_k^2$$ The optimal execution trajectory minimizes the utility function balancing expected cost and risk aversion $\lambda$: $$\min_{\{x_k\}} U(x) = E[x] + \lambda V[x] = \frac{1}{2} \gamma X_0^2 + \frac{\eta}{\tau} \sum_{k=1}^N (x_{k-1} - x_k)^2 + \lambda \sigma^2 \tau \sum_{k=1}^N x_k^2$$ ### 2.4 Continuous Formulation via Euler-Lagrange In the continuous-time limit ($\tau \to 0$), the functional is: $$J[x] = \int_0^T \left( \eta \dot{x}^2(t) + \lambda \sigma^2 x^2(t) \right) dt$$ The Euler-Lagrange condition $\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} = 0$ yields the second-order linear differential equation: $$\eta \ddot{x}(t) - \lambda \sigma^2 x(t) = 0 \implies \ddot{x}(t) - \kappa^2 x(t) = 0$$ where the urgency parameter $\kappa$ is defined as: $$\kappa = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda \sigma^2}{\eta}}$$ Subject to boundary conditions $x(0) = X_0$ and $x(T) = 0$, the exact analytical solution is: $$x(t) = X_0 \frac{\sinh(\kappa (T - t))}{\sinh(\kappa T)}$$ In discrete form for interval $k \in \{0, \dots, N\}$: $$x_k = X_0 \frac{\sinh(\kappa (T - t_k))}{\sinh(\kappa T)}, \quad n_k = x_{k-1} - x_k$$ When risk aversion $\lambda \to 0$ (risk-neutral), $\kappa \to 0$, reducing smoothly to a linear TWAP: $$\lim_{\kappa \to 0} x_k = X_0 \left(1 - \frac{t_k}{T}\right)$$ --- ## 3. Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) Engine The VWAP engine allocates slice sizes in strict proportion to expected intraday liquidity distributions, matching the historical U-shaped intraday volume profile. ### 3.1 Intraday Volume Profile Model The continuous intraday volume fraction density $w(t)$ on $t \in [0, 1]$ (normalized trading day) is modeled by a bimodal polynomial: $$w(t) = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 (t - 0.5)^2 + \alpha_2 (t - 0.5)^4 + \beta_1 e^{-\frac{t^2}{2\sigma_{open}^2}} + \beta_2 e^{-\frac{(1 - t)^2}{2\sigma_{close}^2}}$$ where: - High volume concentration occurs at market open ($t \in [0.0, 0.15]$) and close ($t \in [0.85, 1.0]$). - A liquidity trough persists during midday lunch hours ($t \in [0.4, 0.6]$). For $N$ discrete execution bins: $$w_k = \int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_k} w(t) dt, \quad W = \sum_{k=1}^N w_k$$ Child slice sizing for bin $k$: $$n_k^{VWAP} = X_0 \cdot \frac{w_k}{W}$$ ### 3.2 Dynamic Recalibration on Volume Spikes When an empirical volume spike $V_{actual}(t) > (1 + \theta) V_{expected}(t)$ is observed at step $m$: $$n_k^{recalibrated} = x_{m-1} \cdot \frac{w_k'}{\sum_{j=m}^N w_j'}, \quad \forall k \ge m$$ --- ## 4. Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP) with Randomized Jitter Linear slicing without randomization produces deterministic order flow vulnerable to predatory high-frequency algorithms (e.g. queue jumping, front-running, and liquidity withdrawal). ### 4.1 Linear Baseline $$n_{base} = \frac{X_0}{N}, \quad \Delta t = \frac{T}{N}$$ ### 4.2 Bounded Stochastic Sizing Jitter For a jitter parameter $\delta \in [0, 0.5]$: $$\tilde{n}_k = n_{base} \cdot (1 + \epsilon_k), \quad \epsilon_k \sim \mathcal{U}(-\delta, \delta)$$ To preserve zero leakage ($\sum_{k=1}^N n_k = X_0$), residual normalization is enforced: $$n_k^{TWAP} = \tilde{n}_k \cdot \frac{X_0}{\sum_{j=1}^N \tilde{n}_j}$$ ### 4.3 Randomized Dispatch Interval Jitter $$\tau_k = t_k + \Delta t \cdot \zeta_k, \quad \zeta_k \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.3, 0.3)$$ --- ## 5. Slippage Analytics & Implementation Shortfall Implementation Shortfall (Perold, 1988) decomposes total execution cost against the Arrival Price benchmark: 1. **Arrival Price Benchmark**: $S_0$ (mid-market price at parent order submission). 2. **Effective Execution Price**: $$\bar{P}_{exec} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^N n_k P_k}{\sum_{k=1}^N n_k}$$ 3. **Implementation Shortfall (IS)**: $$\text{IS} = (\bar{P}_{exec} - S_0) \cdot X_0 \quad (\text{for BUY})$$ $$\text{IS} = (S_0 - \bar{P}_{exec}) \cdot X_0 \quad (\text{for SELL})$$ 4. **Slippage in Basis Points (bps)**: $$\text{Slippage (bps)} = \frac{|\bar{P}_{exec} - S_0|}{S_0} \times 10,000$$ --- ## 6. Cryptographic Audit Trail (SHA-256 Hash Chaining) Every parent order and child slice execution is appended to a tamper-evident hash-chained execution tape: $$H_0 = \text{SHA-256}(\text{ParentOrderGenesis})$$ $$H_k = \text{SHA-256}(H_{k-1} \,\|\, k \,\|\, \text{SliceID} \,\|\, n_k \,\|\, P_k \,\|\, \text{Venue} \,\|\, t_k)$$ Any retroactive alteration of execution prices or quantities breaks the hash chain, satisfying SEC Rule 611 and MiFID II RTS 25 clock synchronization and order tracking requirements. --- ## 7.
